Overview

Purpose

Audience Need: PT Pathway Tracker is a resource website designed to help patients and caregivers understand and perform common physical therapy exercises safely at home. It provides clear, step-by-step instructions, safety notes, and progress-tracking suggestions so users gain confidence performing prescribed exercises outside of clinic visits.

What Visitors Want: Visitors will come to the site seeking reliable guidance for rehabilitation after injury or surgery, quick reminders for exercises prescribed by their therapist, and explanations about why each movement matters. They will expect accessible language, helpful images or short diagrams, and practical tips to avoid common mistakes.

Primary Tasks: The main activities users will complete include reading exercise instructions, viewing images demonstrating form, downloading or printing simple exercise sheets, and finding additional recovery resources such as mobility tips or referrals to local clinics.

Why This Site: This site stands out by focusing on clarity and patient empowerment: each exercise includes purpose, steps, common errors, modifications, and approximate sets/reps so users can safely practice at home. The content aims to complement — not replace — professional guidance from a licensed therapist.

Content Structure: I will organize the site into a clear home page and two child pages: one focused on common exercises and progressions, and another focused on recovery resources, safety, and lifestyle tips that support rehabilitation. Each page will contain meaningful text, images, and links between the pages for easy navigation.

Tone & Accessibility: The writing will be empathetic, concise, and action-oriented. Text will be broken into short paragraphs with bolded topic labels for scanning, and images will be optimized for fast loading and accompanied by descriptive alt text for accessibility.

Audience

The target audience for PT Pathway Tracker is patients recovering from injuries or undergoing physical therapy treatment at home. This includes individuals seeking clearer instructions on exercises prescribed by their therapists, those wanting to understand the purpose behind their exercises, and people looking for a reliable resource to track their recovery progress.

Content

Home page

Elevator Pitch: PT Pathway Tracker helps patients continue their rehabilitation at home with clear, trustworthy exercise instructions, images, and recovery tips. The home page introduces the site's purpose, highlights the two main areas of the site — Exercises and Recovery — and points visitors toward quick-start resources.

Welcome Message: Welcome to PT Pathway Tracker. If you've been given exercises by a physical therapist, this site will help you understand why each exercise matters and how to perform them correctly. Use the navigation to explore specific exercises or recovery strategies that support healing.

Exercises

Overview: This page lists common therapeutic exercises grouped by body region (e.g., shoulder, knee, lower back) and includes clear instructions, recommended repetitions, and safety modifications. Each exercise entry will explain the goal, the step-by-step movement, and common form cues.

Example Exercise Copy: Glute Bridge — Purpose: activate the gluteal muscles and improve pelvic stability. Steps: lie on your back with knees bent, press through heels, lift hips to form a straight line from shoulders to knees, hold 2–3 seconds, lower slowly. Reps: 2–3 sets of 10–15. Modifications: place hands on hips for feedback; progress by adding a heel raise or single-leg bridge.

Recovery

Overview: The Recovery page offers information about pacing, pain vs. harm, sleep and nutrition tips for healing, and suggestions for when to contact a healthcare provider. It will also include links to printable progress logs and reputable external resources.

Sample Recovery Tip: Sleep and consistency are powerful recovery tools. Aim for regular sleep windows, hydrate well, and include gentle mobility in your daily routine. If pain increases sharply or you experience new neurological symptoms, stop the activity and contact your provider.
